ABOUT POPCRAFT

A concept for keeping good ideas alive.

Popcraft started as a question: what if a music-AI company's B2C products didn't quietly get discontinued the moment focus shifted elsewhere? We think Splash proved the concept — Popcraft is the plan to keep proving it.

THE STORY SO FAR

From Popgun, to Splash, to Popcraft.

2016 — Popgun Labs

An AI-music startup with a big idea

Popgun Labs set out to build AI that could genuinely understand and make music — not just imitate it.

2019 — Splash launches

The idea meets a consumer audience

Splash brought that research to everyday creators through games, a desktop production suite, and a growing roster of in-game Stars.

2020 — Splash on Roblox

21 million players find their sound

Splash's music-and-skate world became one of the largest AI-music experiences of its time.

Today — Popcraft

A commitment, not just a rebrand

Popcraft carries Splash's product line forward under one explicit rule: every B2C product ships with a support commitment attached, so nothing gets discontinued out from under the people using it.

FACE OF POPCRAFT

Meet your guide through the workshop.

Every Splash generation had a character who showed people around — Popcraft's is still being cast. This card is wired up and ready for whoever that ends up being; swap in final art and copy whenever it's locked.

THE POPCRAFT PLEDGE

Three commitments, in plain language.

1. Ship with a support plan

No B2C product launches without a public commitment to how long it'll be maintained.

2. Announce before we sunset

If something ever has to be retired, creators hear about it early, with a real migration path — not a shutdown notice.

3. Keep improving the AI, not just the AI

Model upgrades roll out to existing products first. New features come after, not instead.
